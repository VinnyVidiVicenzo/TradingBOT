  'Best on one path' picked 0% crypto / 0% stocks / 100% cash.
  'Best across many paths' picked 0% crypto / 10% stocks / 90% cash.
  THEY DISAGREE. That disagreement is the whole point: a weight tuned to one
  specific history is not the same thing as a weight that holds up broadly.

  IMPORTANT CAVEAT ON THE RANKING ABOVE: 'gain per unit of worst drawdown'
  mechanically rewards near-zero-volatility mixes, because dividing by an
  almost-zero drawdown inflates the ratio even for a small gain. That is why
  cash-heavy mixes top the list -- it is a true statement about the RATIO, not
  a claim that they grew your money the most. For contrast, here is the same
  grid ranked by plain median ending value instead:

  rank    crypto  stocks    cash  median end  lose money
  1         100%      0%      0%         561         21%
  2          90%     10%     -0%         543         19%
  3          80%     20%     -0%         536         17%
  4          90%      0%     10%         529         20%
  5          80%     10%     10%         523         18%

  Ranked by growth, more crypto climbs the list; ranked by gain-per-drawdown,
  cash does. Which ranking matters more is a question about how much risk is
  acceptable, not a question this arithmetic can answer by itself.


C. FOR A BLENDED PORTFOLIO, DOES SPREADING OUT THE BUYING MATTER MORE?

  Earlier tests found spreading out PURE crypto purchases made no reliable
  difference. Repeating that test for a 90% stock / 10% crypto blend, added
  to gradually over 12 weeks vs all at once, every start month, 1-year hold.

  Staged beat lump-sum in 22 of 60 start months (37%).
  Typical (median) result -- lump sum: 231   staged: 230
  Same conclusion as for pure crypto: spreading out the buying does not
  reliably help, even once most of the money is in something steadier.
