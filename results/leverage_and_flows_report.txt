3. THE BIGGEST DRAWDOWNS IN OUR OWN DATA, AND WHAT DROVE THEM

  (from results/risk_profile.csv and event_study.py; qualitative,
   cross-referenced against public reporting, not measured here)

  2025-10-10 to 2025-10-11  (BTC -12.5% close-to-low in our own data (~-14% intraday, widely reported))
    THE LARGEST LEVERAGE LIQUIDATION EVENT ON RECORD. Trump's announcement of a 100% tariff on Chinese imports hit equities and commodities first; crypto was sitting near record-high open interest and crowded long positioning and became, in one analyst's words, 'the perfect pressure point.' Over $19bn in leveraged positions were forcibly closed, more than 1.6 million trading accounts liquidated -- about 9x the February 2025 crash and 19x the FTX collapse. Cross-checked against this project's own Binance.US data: BTCUSDT closed $124,798 on Oct 6 and fell to a low of $109,278 by Oct 11, independently confirming an event of this scale really happened, on these dates, on this exchange -- not just in the news reports. Sources: CoinDesk, Forbes, CCN, FTI Consulting (links in results/reference_verification.md addendum below).

  2021-05-19 to 2021-05-23  (BTC -30% in 4 days)
    China's mining-ban headline hit a market already record-long on leverage. $8.6bn in leveraged positions were forcibly closed in 24 hours -- a record at the time, since surpassed by October 2025 above. Amplified an initial news-driven drop into a much larger one.

  2022-05-07 to 2022-05-12  (LUNA itself fell essentially to zero)
    Not a futures-leverage cascade -- a stablecoin (UST) losing its peg triggered a bank-run-style redemption spiral. It matters for this project's data because it triggered broad de-risking and leveraged liquidations across the wider market in its wake, visible in results/risk_profile.csv as one of the worst stretches in the whole six-year window.

  2022-11-08 to 2022-11-09  (FTX collapse, BTC -14% in a day)
    FTX's own affiliated trading firm (Alameda) was a major leveraged market participant; its insolvency and FTX's own halted withdrawals forced liquidations across every exchange with exposure to either firm.

  Pattern across all four: the INITIAL trigger was news, tariffs, or a
  stablecoin failure -- not leverage itself. But every time, a market already
  carrying a lot of leveraged, one-directional betting turned that trigger into
  a much larger move than the news alone would suggest, because forced closures
  beget more forced closures. The October 2025 event is the clearest illustration:
  the trigger (a tariff announcement) had nothing to do with crypto at all.
