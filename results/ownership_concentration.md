# Who holds it: ownership concentration (rich lists)

Source: BitInfoCharts top-100 pages, fetched 2026-09-27. Free, less reliable
than paid on-chain analytics, and needs careful reading: the biggest "addresses"
are mostly exchanges and custodians holding coins for millions of customers, so
concentration by address overstates concentration by person.

## Dogecoin -- highly concentrated

- Top 10 addresses: about **57.6%** of all DOGE
- Top 100 addresses: about **74.8%**
- 7 of the top 10 are labelled exchanges or known entities: Robinhood (17.4% alone),
  Upbit, Binance (three wallets), Cryptsy, Bithumb.
- Even allowing for exchanges, three unlabelled wallets in the top 12 hold large
  shares. A small number of holders could move the price sharply.

## Bitcoin -- far less concentrated (figures partly inconsistent)

- From the balance table: about 88 addresses holding over 10,000 BTC each control
  roughly **15%** of all Bitcoin; about 1,900 addresses with 1,000-10,000 BTC hold
  about 21% more.
- The same page's summary line gave "top 100 = 7.4%", which contradicts the table.
  The table is used here; treat the exact figure as uncertain.
- 5 of the top 10 are labelled: Binance (several cold wallets), Robinhood, Bitfinex,
  Tether. Much of the rest is exchange and ETF-custodian holdings on behalf of others.

## Ethereum -- not obtained

- The rich-list page returned a server error on two attempts.
- Known structurally (not measured here): Ethereum's largest single "holder" is the
  staking deposit contract, which pools coins from many independent stakers, so
  raw rich-list figures for ETH would overstate concentration anyway.

## Reading it

Dogecoin's ownership is concentrated enough to be a real risk in its own right.
Bitcoin's is broad by crypto standards. Neither figure accounts for coins that have
been lost for good (Bitcoin especially), which makes the effective supply smaller.
